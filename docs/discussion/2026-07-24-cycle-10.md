## Lessons we learnt

- **Observation**: global information wins when injected as MOVES over
  the incumbent (cut surgery) or INPUT (simplification), and loses as
  search control (cycles 9) or as exhaustive enumeration (056) or as
  population policy (055). **Implication**: the paper's algorithm story
  has two pillars — simplify-then-anneal and waist surgery — both
  "feed the local engine globally," plus the composite's routing/racing.
- **Observation**: the SA frontier's dominant cut is never globally
  minimal, yet SA cannot fix it (one-leaf moves), and only on separable
  networks does fixing it pay. **Implication**: a clean structural
  characterization of WHERE local search is sufficient (expanders) vs
  where global cut moves are necessary (separable networks) — paper
  material bridging both arcs.
- **Observation**: rqc_97_m24 has now repelled 047 (−0.025), 049
  (median), and 054 (confirmation) — three mechanisms grazing without
  confirming. **Implication**: either its reference row is lucky-strong
  or the instance sits at its effective optimum; a bounds thread
  (minor-lifting, from the Tamaki discussion) is the right tool, not
  more optimizer attempts.

### Literature check

FM/KL cut refinement applied to the dominant contraction of an
SA-optimal tree, with the always-improvable-waist finding, appears
novel; standard multilevel partitioners use FM to BUILD trees (which
failed in 005/037/041) — the difference is surgery on an already-good
tree. The block-DP blow-up measurement quantifies a folklore
expectation; the outer-product counterexample sharpens netcon-style
pruning claims ([pfeifer_2013_faster]).
