## Lessons we learnt

- **Observation**: 004 passed the speed bar with what is essentially the
  baseline algorithm plus scheduling hygiene (portfolio floor capped at 15%
  budget, plain TreeSA doubling ramp, self-exit at 0.85×budget, atomic
  writes), while every algorithmically novel draft scored below it.
  **Root cause**: baseline TreeSA's default parameters overshoot — it
  reaches its converged tc in ≤ T/4 on all 9 dev families, so the measured
  budgets T(g) contain ~4× slack; harvesting that slack requires only an
  anytime schedule that never wastes or overruns budget, not a better
  search. **Evidence**: 004's per-instance table (T/4 tc identical to
  full-budget baseline on 8/9 graphs, reg3_250 46.1 vs 47.0); 006's LOG
  (budget-bug fixes moved score more than its DP); holdout replication.
  *Confirmed.* **Implication**: the honest headline claim is "TreeSA-class
  quality at 4× less optimizer time, validated on unseen instances" — a
  scheduling result, not a search result. The +1.0 quality bar remains open
  and is the real research target for any future cycle.
- **Observation**: every SA-family attempt (001, 002, 003, 008, 010) failed
  *only* on reg3_250 at reduced budgets, overwhelmingly via the sc-cap
  guard (sc 34–36 vs cap 35) rather than tc. **Root cause**: omeco TreeSA's
  default score function targets sc_target=20 with weight 1 — on a graph
  whose optimal sc is ~33 the sc pressure is almost inactive, and
  short-budget anneals emit whatever sc the trajectory happens to have; the
  attempts inherited this objective. **Evidence**: identical failure
  signature across five independent implementations; 008's LOG (sc-floor
  tracking halved but did not eliminate violations). *Confirmed.*
  **Implication**: any future attempt must treat sc as a hard constraint in
  the search objective (reject or penalize above baseline_sc+2 explicitly),
  not as a soft target; this is a one-line objective change worth one
  improvement attempt.
- **Observation**: all three constructive drafts (005 min-cut, 009 beam,
  007 elimination-order) matched TreeSA on structured graphs but lost by
  4–17 tc on random 3-regular instances, worsening with size. **Root
  cause**: reg3 graphs are expanders — no balanced separators (005), no
  low-width elimination orders reachable by monotone construction (007),
  and beam-width diversity cannot represent the exponentially many
  near-optimal partial states (009); only iterative refinement over full
  trees finds their low-width structure. **Evidence**: 005's reg3_250 tc 59
  vs 47; 007's plateau at 64 vs 47 even at 8 s; 009's 66–73 vs 47 with sc
  blow-ups. *Confirmed.* **Implication**: blacklist pure constructive
  methods for expander-dominated suites; their value, if any, is as seeds.
- **Observation**: 001 and 008 both found tc ≈ 41–44 on reg3_250 at full
  budget — beating the baseline's 47 by 3–6 log2 units — yet scored
  negative overall. **Root cause**: their reg3_250 wins were erased by
  sc-cap failures at reduced budgets (the previous lesson), and by the
  scoring design in which small-budget pairs weigh equally with full-budget
  pairs. **Evidence**: 001's LOG (Δtc ≈ +4.8 at T on reg3_250), 008's LOG
  (tc 41–44 when sc permits), validator errors confined to sc.
  *Confirmed.* **Implication**: the quality-win path (+1.0 mean) is
  plausibly reachable by combining 008/001's coarse-move refinement with a
  hard sc constraint — the top-ranked candidate change for a next cycle.

Ranked most-important change: fix the sc handling in the search objective;
second: subtree-shift/basin-hop refinement on top of 004's scheduling.

### Evidence carried forward

- Bar met by 004: dev speed_win + holdout speed_win with generalization
  guard, zero errors (reports in `.worktrees/attempt-004/report*.json`).
- Baseline budgets contain ~4× slack (004, confirmed on holdout).
- reg3 expanders defeat constructive methods (005/007/009 refuted) —
  blacklist: recursive bisection, elimination-order SA with
  smallest-intermediate decode, beam search without sc-awareness.
- Coarse SA moves (subtree-shift, basin-hop perturbation) beat baseline
  TreeSA by 3–6 tc on reg3_250 at full budget (001, 008) — promoted
  direction, blocked only by sc handling.
- sc-cap at small budgets is the systematic killer (5 attempts) — root
  cause identified in TreeSA's soft sc_target=20 objective.
- Holdout: 1 query spent (004, passed). Dev-overfit risk for 004 is low —
  its mechanism (schedule hygiene) is instance-agnostic and replicated on
  unseen sizes.

### Literature check

The 004 result matches the anytime-protocol point in Gray–Kourtis
[gray_2020_hyper] (optimizers should be compared as best-so-far curves, and
default budgets are rarely on the knee of the curve); no paper in
`.knowledge/` claims the specific "TreeSA defaults overshoot 4×" schedule
result for OMEinsumContractionOrders-style TreeSA, so the claim appears
novel at the engineering-note level, not the algorithmic level. The
001/008 reg3_250 gains are consistent with the subtree-transfer results of
[anon_2025_optimizing]; a quality-win write-up would need a head-to-head
with cotengra's SA mode (pinned, not yet run — see CATALOG.md). Shelved
insights: none to promote (all six were Selected).
