# Bond-dimension scaling of a local-refinement advantage over hyperoptimized tensor-network contraction on Sycamore like topologies

**Authors:** Rubén Darío Guerrero

**Citation:** preprint, 2026

**arXiv:** [2604.25532](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25532)

## Abstract

We identify a missing local-refinement stage in the cotengra tensor-network contraction pipeline and show that its impact grows monotonically with bond dimension on the \emph{connectivity graph} of Sycamore-like topologies. Appending a nearest-neighbor interchange (NNI) search to the \cotengra{} output at matched 8-s wallclock yields a median \emph{predicted} cost-model gap $\Delta\fT$ at $n{=}500$ that grows monotonically and approximately linearly in $\chi$, from $\sim\!15$~bits at $\chi{=}2$ to $\sim\!116$~bits at $\chi{=}16$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:chi_sweep}), with the refiner winning on $25/25$ seeds at every tested $\chi$. Two control families -- random $3$-regular and QAOA $p{=}2$ interaction graphs -- show median $|\Delta\fT| \leq 0.71$~bits across both controls at every $\chi$, with refiner win rate falling toward chance as $\chi$ grows; the signal is topology-specific, not a generic refinement-budget effect. An ablation establishes that refinement itself, not the four-axis Pareto acceptance rule, drives the gain ($|\Delta\fT| \lesssim 0.1$ bits between scalar and Pareto arms at $\chi{=}2$). The Sycamore-circuit envelope (App.~\ref{em:sec:results:syccirc}) reports the corresponding refinement on actual random circuits at depths $m \in \{4, 6, 8, 10, 12\}$, where the refiner wins on $5/5$ instances at every depth. The advantage is therefore largest precisely in the bond-dimension regime relevant to physical contraction.

_Introduction._ —We report that on the _connectivity graph_ of Sycamore-like tensor-network topologies, a single local-refinement stage appended to the production hyperoptimizer `cotengra-hyper` [1]
captures contraction-order structure that hyperoptimization misses, with the captured predicted
FLOP-cost reduction growing monotonically and approximately linearly in bond dimension _χ_ .
Classical simulation of quantum circuits at Sycamore scale reduces to contracting a tensor network, and the contraction order sets the FLOP count by many orders of magnitude [1–3]. The
production tool for contraction-order search, `cotengra-hyper`, drives a hypergraph-partitioning
backend [4] under Bayesian hyperparameter search over many randomized greedy seeds and returns
the scalar-best tree. It is the search engine behind several large-scale re-simulations of supremacyclass circuits [5–7].
`cotengra-hyper` allocates its wallclock budget to _exploration_ : drawing fresh random seeds,
each evaluated end-to-end. No local refinement of the final tree is performed. This is deliberate,
and on topologies whose contraction order is well-captured by hypergraph partitioning (random
regular graphs, QAOA [8]) it is appropriate. We report that on 2D-grid topologies with diagonal
couplings—the connectivity class of the Google Sycamore device [9]—the assumption fails by a
margin that grows steeply with bond dimension. A _single local-refinement stage_ appended to
the `cotengra-hyper` output yields a median ∆ _fT_ at _n_ =500 that grows from 14 _._ 7 bits at _χ_ =2 to
116 _._ 3 bits at _χ_ =16, with all 25 of 25 seeds improved at every tested _χ_ (Fig. 2). (A previously
reported 128 _×_ figure at _χ_ =2 stands as a conservative lower bound of the present _χ_ =2 cell; see
App. B, _Protocol delta_ .)

_Related work._ —Local refinement of contraction orders has appeared in the contraction-tree
literature in several forms. `cotengra-hyper` itself exposes simulated-annealing modes within
`ctg.HyperOptimizer` [1], but they operate on the hyperparameter space of the random-restart
sampler rather than as an nni local search appended to a fixed seed. Sliced-contraction work [6, 10]
reduces peak-memory and total cost on Sycamore-class circuits via index slicing and joint slicing/ordering search, complementary to the order-only refinement studied here. Reference [5] reuses
partial contraction subtrees across batched amplitudes, exploiting structural reuse rather than local
sub-optimality of the order itself. The nni move predates the contraction-tree literature, originating in phylogenetics [11, 12], where nni-based hill-climbing has been the workhorse of tree search for
decades; we transfer that move set to the contraction-tree setting and specialize it to the four-axis
cost vector defined below.

Geometrically, each diagonal coupler in the Sycamore-like lattice closes a 4-cycle with two adjacent grid edges (the diagonal plus the two horizontal/vertical edges sharing its endpoints), and
adjacent diagonals share endpoints to form chains of overlapping 4-cycles. KaHyPar’s hypergraph
cut [4] optimizes a global edge-cut weight: it can choose _which_ edges of a 4-cycle to cut (placing cycle vertices on opposite sides of the partition) but not the relative _ordering_ in which the
within-cycle pairwise contractions occur within each part. The resulting contraction tree therefore
inherits a degenerate ordering of cycle-internal contractions that is well-resolved by the local-move
neighborhood: an nni swap across a 4-cycle changes which pair of cycle vertices is contracted
first, and at uniform bond _χ_ the difference in peak intermediate size between the two orderings is
exactly log2 _χ_ per shared edge resolved (the per-edge _χ_ -factor recovered in the _Discussion_ section).
The high observed dominator density on Sycamore-like ( _∼_ 15%) is the population statistic of this
under-determination across the many overlapping 4-cycles of the diagonal-coupled grid, and the
per-swap _χ_ -factor is what makes that population accumulate to the linear-in- _χ_ slope of Fig. 2.

_Discussion._ —The operative lever in contraction-order optimization on 2D-structured tensor
networks is budget allocation between exploration (random seeds) and exploitation (local refinement). `cotengra-hyper` spends its wallclock on exploration; on Sycamore-like topologies this
leaves a Pareto headroom of _∼_ 15% of dominating nni neighbors on the table at the end of the
search ( _χ_ =2). A single refinement stage captures it at matched total wallclock. The geometric
picture sharpens at larger _χ_ : each captured edge swap removes a multiplicative _χ_ -factor from the
contracted intermediate, so the log-cost gain accumulates proportionally to _χ_, while the controls’
near-Pareto-local seeds offer nothing to compound. Quantitatively, if a swap removes one shared
edge of bond _χ_ from a peak intermediate of pre-swap size 2 _[k]_, the resulting ∆ _fT_ contribution at that
node is log2 _χ_ . Schematically, summing over the _∼_ 15% dominating neighbors across the _N_ int internal nodes of a depth-balanced tree at _n_ =500 gives ∆ _fT ∼_ 0 _._ 15 _·N_ int _·_ log2 _χ_ as an order-of-magnitude
estimate, recovering the observed slope on Sycamore-like (Table 1: ∆ _fT_ grows by 32 _,_ 33 _,_ 37 bits
across the three doublings _χ_ : 2 _→_ 4 _,_ 4 _→_ 8 _,_ 8 _→_ 16, i.e. _∼_ 34 bits per doubling, dominated by deeper
nodes whose peak operates at higher _k_ but whose dominator density is comparable). This is consistent with the observed approximately linear growth of median ∆ _fT_ with _χ_ on Sycamore-like
(Fig. 2) and the absence of any analogous trend on the controls.
The Pareto-versus-scalar ablation shows that the multi-objective formalism is not required to
realize the _fT_ reduction at _χ_ =2: a single-objective nni-local search appended to `cotengra-hyper`
captures structure that hyperoptimization misses, and the cleaner story carries through the _χ_
sweep. The Pareto formalism contributes a deterministic multi-axis safety guarantee and a reproducible termination certificate at zero measured _fT_ cost; this becomes load-bearing at larger _χ_ or
tighter memory/precision budgets where _fS_, _fσ_, or _fε_ begin to bind (see Limitations), but is not
a precondition for the predicted FLOP-cost reduction itself.
As a falsification test, an alternate logit best-response acceptance rule converges to bit-identical
trees on Sycamore-like at every _χ_ (Fig. C8). The headline _χ_ -scaling result is therefore independent
of the choice of acceptance rule on the topology where it matters.
Natural extensions include heavy-hexagon [15] and 3D topologies, joint slicing-and-ordering
optimization [6, 16], and direct integration of a local refinement phase into `cotengra-hyper` .
The prescription for practitioners is direct: any `cotengra-hyper` -based pipeline on 2D-structured
tensor networks stands to gain in predicted FLOP cost by appending the refinement stage we
describe, with the gain growing steeply in the bond dimension. The stage requires no additional
wallclock budget beyond the 8 s refinement allowance and no modification of the `cotengra-hyper`
front end. We emphasize that the multi-order-of-magnitude figures at large _χ_ are ratios of _cost_
_models_, not of end-to-end executed contractions; whether the predicted cost reduction translates
into wallclock reduction on a given FLOP-bound contraction kernel remains to be measured.

##### B Extended method


_Seed._ The refiner consumes the `cotengra-hyper` [1] output tree in its nested-tuple form and
translates it to a flat parent–left–right integer encoding of length 2(2 _n−_ 1) suitable for GPU transfer.
All main-text experiments use `cotengra-hyper` at its default hyperoptimizer settings, sampling
over _{_ `greedy` _,_ `kahypar` _,_ `labels` _}_ backends. Baselines other than `cotengra-hyper` ( `opt` ~~`e`~~ `insum` greedy [19] and `cotengra-hyper` -greedy) are evaluated in Fig. 5 but not used as seed sources in
the main result.
nni _enumerator._ A nearest-neighbor interchange move [11] at an internal edge ( _p, c_ ) of the
contraction tree exchanges one of _c_ ’s two children with _p_ ’s other child. The neighborhood has
4( _n−_ 2) elements per tree. The library primitive `neighbors` ~~`w`~~ `ith` `trees` ( _τ_ ) applies and reverts
each move in place on a shared workspace, returning both the serialized tree and the four-axis
score of every neighbor in one call. All acceptance-rule arms (Pareto, scalar _fT_ ) consume the same
primitive, so the only experimental variable between arms is the acceptance rule.
_GPU-parallel evaluator._ The fused CUDA kernel dedicates one thread block to each candidate
tree and one warp to each post-order subtree level. Open-set bitsets open( _v_ ) are stored as 32word (2048-bit) packed bitmaps in shared memory; the open( _L_ ) _△_ open( _R_ ) recursion (symmetric

optimizer’s cost evaluator itself runs in FP64.
